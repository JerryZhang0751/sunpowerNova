## What to check before buying

### 1. Battery chemistry
Ask whether it uses **LiFePO₄ / LFP** cells. LFP is generally preferred for home storage because it is safer, durable, and commonly used by major battery brands.

### 2. Usable capacity
Confirm the difference between:
- **Nominal capacity** — e.g. 10 kWh advertised
- **Usable capacity** — what you can actually use

Some cheaper systems advertise total capacity but provide less usable energy.

### 3. Inverter compatibility
Check whether SunHestia works with your inverter or requires a specific hybrid inverter. Ask for a written compatibility list covering brands such as:
- Victron
- Deye / Sunsynk
- Solis
- Growatt
- GoodWe
- Fronius
- SMA

### 4. Warranty
A serious home battery should have:
- Around **10 years warranty**
- Clear cycle-life rating
- Minimum retained capacity guarantee, often around **60–80% after warranty period**
- Written warranty terms from the manufacturer, not only the installer

If the warranty is vague, avoid it.

### 5. Certifications
Ask for proof of relevant safety and grid certifications, such as:
- IEC 62619
- UN38.3
- CE / UKCA where applicable
- UL 1973 or UL 9540 / UL 9540A, especially in North America
- Local grid approval if it connects to the electrical system

### 6. After-sales support
This is especially important. Ask:
- Who handles warranty claims?
- Is there a local distributor?
- Are replacement battery modules available?
- Is monitoring software maintained?
- What happens if the installer goes out of business?

### 7. Price comparison
If SunHestia is much cheaper than known brands, find out why. Compare it against better-known options such as:
- Tesla Powerwall
- Enphase IQ Battery
- BYD Battery-Box
- Pylontech
- Huawei Luna
- Sonnen
- SolarEdge Home Battery
- GivEnergy
- FoxESS
- Dyness

## Red flags

Avoid or delay purchase if:
- There is no official datasheet
- Warranty terms are not publicly available
- The installer cannot provide certifications
- The brand has little or no independent review history
- The battery management system compatibility is unclear
- The company has no local service presence
- The quoted price seems unusually low

## Bottom line

**SunHestia may be acceptable only if it is backed by a reputable installer, has proper certifications, a clear 10-year warranty, and documented inverter compatibility.** Without those, I would choose a more established home battery brand, even if it costs more.

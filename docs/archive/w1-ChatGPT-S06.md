Yes—a home battery **can power a house overnight**, but whether it can power *your whole house* depends mainly on:

1. **Battery usable capacity** — measured in kWh
2. **Your overnight electricity use**
3. **Whether you run big loads** like HVAC, electric heat, water heater, oven, dryer, well pump, or EV charging

### Quick rule of thumb

A typical home battery has around **10–15 kWh** of usable storage.

- **Essential loads only** — fridge, lights, Wi‑Fi, outlets, some fans:
  **Usually yes**, one battery can often last overnight.
- **Whole house with moderate use** — fridge, lights, electronics, some HVAC:
  **Maybe**, depending on climate and appliance use.
- **Whole house with heavy AC/heat, EV charging, electric water heating, etc.:**
  **Often no**, unless you have multiple batteries.

### Example

If your house uses **1 kW on average overnight** for 10 hours:

> 1 kW × 10 hours = 10 kWh

A battery with **13.5 kWh usable capacity** could likely cover that.

But if your AC, heat pump, or electric heater pushes usage to **3–5 kW**, the same battery may only last a few hours.

### Typical answer

For many homes, **one battery can power essential circuits overnight**.
To power the **entire house overnight**, especially with heating or cooling, you may need **two or more batteries**.

If you have solar panels, the battery can recharge the next day, making overnight backup much more practical.

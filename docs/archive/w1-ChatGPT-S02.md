**Short answer: yes, sometimes — but the battery is the part that needs careful justification.** Solar panels alone are often a strong investment; adding a battery is worth it mainly when your grid electricity is expensive, export payments are low, you use power in the evening/night, or you can exploit time-of-use/dynamic tariffs.

### When a solar + battery system is usually worth it

It is more likely to pay off if:

- **Your import price is much higher than your export tariff.** The battery saves you the difference between what you would pay the utility and what you would earn exporting surplus.
- **You consume a lot after sunset** — cooking, EV charging, heat pump, air conditioning, evening appliances.
- **Net metering is weak, being phased out, or unavailable.**
- **You have dynamic/time-of-use tariffs.** Charging from cheap solar or cheap off-peak grid power and discharging during expensive evening hours can improve the economics.
- **You value backup power or energy independence**, not just payback.

Without a battery, households often self-consume only about **25–35%** of their solar generation; a well-sized battery can raise that to around **60–80%** according to the cited guide [1]. Typical residential battery sizes in Europe are roughly **5–15 kWh usable**, depending on consumption and tariff structure [1].

### When it may not be worth it

A battery is often **not** financially attractive if:

- You have **full retail net metering**.
- Your export tariff is close to your import price.
- Your electricity price is low.
- The installed battery cost is high.
- You are rarely home in the evening or your loads are already daytime-heavy.
- The battery is oversized and sits partly unused.

### Quick rule-of-thumb calculation

Use this:

**Annual battery value ≈ kWh shifted per year × (grid import price − export tariff) × battery efficiency**

Example:

- Battery shifts: **2,000 kWh/year**
- Import price: **€0.30/kWh**
- Export tariff: **€0.08/kWh**
- Difference: **€0.22/kWh**
- Battery efficiency: **90%**

Annual saving ≈ 2,000 × €0.22 × 0.9 = **€396/year**

If the battery costs **€5,000 installed**, simple payback is about **12.6 years**. If electricity is €0.40/kWh and export is near zero, payback improves a lot.

The cited source gives indicative paybacks of roughly **6–10 years** for well-sized residential batteries in high-price European markets, especially where dynamic tariffs help [1].

### Sizing advice

Do **not** oversize the battery. A practical starting point:

- Estimate your **daily electricity use**.
- Take the portion used **evening/night** — often 40–60%.
- Size the battery around that usable amount.

For example, a home using **12 kWh/day** might need around **5–8 kWh usable** for self-consumption. The guide suggests many households fall in the **5–15 kWh** range, with larger systems for heat pumps, EVs, or dynamic tariffs [1].

### Bottom line

**Solar panels: usually worth it.**
**Battery: worth it if your price spread is high and you’ll cycle it often.**

If you have high electricity prices, low export compensation, evening loads, and a correctly sized battery, it can make sense. If you have generous net metering or cheap electricity, the battery may be more about backup and independence than financial return.

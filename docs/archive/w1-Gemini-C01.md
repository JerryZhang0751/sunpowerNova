### 3. Recommended "Best System" Configurations

Because solar panels and batteries must work in tandem, they are best paired based on your specific roof layout and utility goals:

#### Option A: The "Ultimate Performance" DC-Coupled System
*   **Panels:** Canadian Solar TOPHiKu6 (465W) [2]
*   **Battery:** Tesla Powerwall 3 [3]
*   **Why it's the best:** By pairing highly efficient panels directly with the Powerwall 3, you can use the Powerwall's built-in hybrid inverter to **DC-couple** the system [3]. DC coupling is highly efficient because power flows straight from the panels to the battery with only one energy conversion, saving you up to 7% in energy losses compared to AC-coupled systems [1].

#### Option B: The "Complex or Shaded Roof" System
*   **Panels:** CertainTeed CT Series or Sirius PV [2]
*   **Inverters:** Enphase Microinverters 
*   **Battery:** Enphase IQ Battery 10C [3]
*   **Why it's the best:** If your roof has partial shading or multiple angles, microinverters optimize production at the individual panel level [3]. The Enphase IQ Battery 10C integrates natively into this ecosystem, providing excellent peak power (14.2 kW) and an industry-leading 15-year warranty [3].

#### Option C: The "Heavy Loads & Whole-Home Backup" System
*   **Panels:** Sirius PV (450W) [2]
*   **Battery:** FranklinWH aPower 2 (Stacked in a 2-unit or 3-unit configuration) [3]
*   **Why it's the best:** For homeowners looking to run energy-heavy appliances like central AC or electric vehicle chargers during a blackout, stacking multiple FranklinWH units offers up to 30–45 kWh of capacity and a massive surge capacity to handle startup motors [1][3].

### Important 2026 Financial & Policy Realities
*   **Federal Incentives:** The U.S. federal **Investment Tax Credit (ITC)** remains active in 2026, offering a **30% tax credit** on the total cost of both solar panels and battery storage (including standalone battery retrofits) [1]. Standalone batteries must be documented as being charged at least 70% from solar to qualify [1].
*   **Net Metering & TOU Rates:** Whether a battery makes financial sense depends entirely on your utility [1]. In states with full retail net metering, a battery is rarely a financial investment and is mostly for backup [1]. However, in states with reduced net metering (like California’s NEM 3.0) or steep Time-of-Use (TOU) rate differentials, storing energy during the day and discharging it during expensive peak evening hours yields massive monthly savings [1][4].

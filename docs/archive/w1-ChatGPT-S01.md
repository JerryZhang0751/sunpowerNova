You can use more of your own solar power by increasing **self-consumption**: shifting electricity use into sunny hours, storing excess, and automating flexible loads.

## 1. Run appliances during solar production hours
Use high-power appliances when your panels are producing most, usually late morning to mid-afternoon.

Good candidates:
- Dishwasher
- Washing machine
- Dryer, if electric
- Pool pump
- Dehumidifier
- Electric oven or induction cooking
- Power tools
- Vacuuming/cleaning appliances

Use built-in delay timers or smart plugs where safe.

## 2. Heat water during the day
If you have an electric water heater, heat pump water heater, or immersion heater, this can absorb a lot of excess solar.

Options:
- Set the water heater schedule to run around midday
- Use a solar diverter/immersion controller to send excess solar to hot water
- Install or switch to a heat pump water heater for much higher efficiency

Hot water is often one of the cheapest “batteries” for solar energy.

## 3. Charge an EV during sunny hours
If you have an electric vehicle, schedule charging for the middle of the day when possible.

Even better:
- Use a smart EV charger that follows solar surplus
- Set a lower charging rate so it matches your solar output rather than pulling from the grid
- Charge more on sunny weekends if the car is home

## 4. Use heating and cooling as thermal storage
Pre-heat or pre-cool your home while solar is abundant.

Examples:
- Run air conditioning a bit more during early afternoon, then reduce use in the evening
- Heat the house during sunny winter hours if you use a heat pump
- Use smart thermostats or schedules

Your home’s walls, floors, and furniture can store some heat or coolness.

## 5. Add a home battery
A battery stores excess daytime solar for evening and nighttime use.

Best if:
- Your export payments are low
- Your evening electricity rates are high
- You want backup power
- You have high evening usage

Before buying, compare the battery cost with your expected savings. Batteries are useful, but not always the fastest payback.

## 6. Use smart controls and energy monitoring
A solar monitoring app or smart meter can show when you are exporting electricity.

Helpful devices:
- Smart plugs
- Smart relays
- Home energy monitors
- Smart EV chargers
- Smart hot water controllers
- Home energy management systems

The goal is to automatically turn on loads only when solar generation is high.

## 7. Stagger appliance use
Avoid running everything at once if that makes demand exceed solar output.

For example:
- Dishwasher at 10:30
- Washing machine at 12:00
- Dryer at 13:30
- EV charging from 11:00–15:00

This keeps more usage within your solar production instead of importing from the grid.

## 8. Move standby and always-on loads to daytime where possible
Some devices can charge during the day and run later:
- Laptops
- Power banks
- Cordless tool batteries
- E-bike batteries
- Robot vacuums
- Portable batteries

Small loads add up over time.

## 9. Consider your electricity tariff
Your best strategy depends on your tariff.

If export rates are low and import rates are high:
**Maximize self-consumption.**

If export rates are generous:
It may be fine to export more.

If you have time-of-use pricing:
Use solar during the day and avoid buying electricity during expensive evening peaks.

## 10. Check if your solar system is sized and configured well
If you regularly export a lot, you may benefit from:
- A battery
- Hot water diversion
- EV charging
- Load shifting
- More controllable appliances

If you rarely export, you may already be using most of your solar.

## Quick checklist

Most effective steps usually are:

1. Run washing machine, dishwasher, dryer, and pool pump during sunny hours
2. Schedule electric water heating for midday
3. Charge EVs from solar when possible
4. Use smart plugs/timers for flexible loads
5. Pre-heat or pre-cool the home with a heat pump or AC
6. Add battery storage if the economics make sense
7. Monitor your export/import data to see what works

A good rule of thumb: **anything that uses heat, cooling, pumping, or charging is a great candidate for using excess solar.**

To increase **self-consumption of solar energy**, you want to use more electricity **while your solar panels are producing**, instead of exporting excess power to the grid. Here are the most effective ways:

## 1. Shift electricity use to daylight hours
Run high-energy appliances when solar production is highest, usually late morning to mid-afternoon.

Examples:
- Washing machine
- Dishwasher
- Tumble dryer
- Electric oven or cooking appliances
- Pool pump
- Dehumidifier
- Water pumps

Using timers or smart plugs can help automate this.

## 2. Install a battery storage system
A home battery stores excess solar power during the day so you can use it in the evening or overnight.

Benefits:
- Higher self-consumption
- Less grid import
- Backup power possible, depending on system
- Better use of solar if export rates are low

This is often the biggest improvement, but it has a higher upfront cost.

## 3. Heat water with excess solar
If you have an electric water heater, you can use solar energy to heat water during the day.

Options include:
- Timer-controlled electric immersion heater
- Smart solar diverter
- Heat pump water heater

A solar diverter sends surplus solar power to your hot water tank instead of exporting it.

## 4. Charge an electric vehicle during the day
If you own an EV, daytime charging can absorb a lot of solar production.

Best options:
- Use a smart EV charger
- Set charging schedules for solar hours
- Use “solar-only” charging mode if available

An EV can significantly increase solar self-consumption.

## 5. Use smart energy management systems
A smart energy manager can automatically control appliances, batteries, EV chargers, and water heaters based on solar production.

It can:
- Detect surplus solar
- Turn devices on/off automatically
- Prioritize battery charging or appliance use
- Reduce grid import

## 6. Pre-heat or pre-cool your home
If you use electric heating or air conditioning, run it while solar output is high.

Examples:
- Cool the house in the afternoon before evening peak
- Heat the house during sunny winter daytime
- Use a heat pump during solar production hours

Your home acts like a thermal battery.

## 7. Match system size to your usage pattern
Oversized solar systems often export more energy unless you have batteries, EV charging, or large daytime loads.

If planning a new system:
- Review your daytime electricity use
- Consider future loads like EVs or heat pumps
- Avoid oversizing unless export payments are worthwhile

## 8. Monitor your solar and consumption data
Use your inverter app, smart meter, or energy monitor to see:
- When solar production peaks
- When your home uses most power
- How much you export
- Which appliances consume the most energy

Once you know your patterns, you can adjust usage more effectively.

## 9. Use appliances with delay-start functions
Many modern appliances allow delayed start. Set them to run around midday.

Good candidates:
- Dishwasher
- Washing machine
- Dryer
- Bread maker
- Slow cooker
- Battery chargers

## 10. Consider tariff optimization
If your utility offers low export payments but high import prices, self-consumption becomes more valuable.

If you have time-of-use tariffs:
- Use solar during expensive grid periods
- Charge batteries when solar is abundant
- Avoid importing during peak prices

## Most effective upgrades, ranked

1. **Load shifting to daytime** — cheapest and easiest
2. **Smart plugs/timers** — low cost, useful automation
3. **Hot water solar diverter** — very effective if you have electric water heating
4. **EV daytime charging** — excellent if you own an EV
5. **Battery storage** — highest impact, higher cost
6. **Smart energy management system** — best for advanced optimization

In short: use more power when the sun is shining, store what you can, and automate as much as possible.

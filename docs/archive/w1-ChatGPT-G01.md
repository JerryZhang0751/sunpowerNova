## What size should you install?

For a typical detached or semi-detached home in Germany:

| Home type | PV size | Battery size |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2–3 people, no EV, gas heating | 6–8 kWp | 5–7 kWh |
| 3–5 people, no heat pump | 8–10 kWp | 7–10 kWh |
| EV or heat pump | 10–15 kWp | 10–15 kWh |
| EV + heat pump + dynamic tariff | 12–20 kWp | 15–25 kWh |

As a rule, don’t oversize the battery unless you have high evening/night consumption, an EV, heat pump, or a dynamic electricity tariff. For many homes with a 4–6 kWp PV system, a 5–10 kWh battery is the practical sweet spot [1].

## Key things to check before signing

1. **Battery chemistry:** choose LFP, not older NMC, for safety and cycle life [2][4].
2. **Inverter compatibility:** this is critical; Huawei and SolarEdge batteries are more ecosystem-locked, while BYD and Pylontech are more flexible [4].
3. **Backup power:** many German solar-battery systems do **not** automatically power the house during an outage unless backup hardware is included.
4. **Installer quality:** in Germany, a good installer matters as much as the brand. Ask for yield simulation, shading analysis, warranty handling, and registration support.
5. **Don’t undersize PV:** roof space is valuable; if the roof is suitable, it is usually better to install more panels now than to expand later.

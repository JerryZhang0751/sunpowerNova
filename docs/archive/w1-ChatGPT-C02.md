## How much battery storage do you need?

For most homes:

- **10–13.5 kWh:** Good for evening use and essential backup
- **20–30 kWh:** Better for whole-home backup
- **30+ kWh:** Large homes, heavy electric loads, or longer outage protection

A typical home battery system is often around **10 kWh**, but the right size depends on your usage, backup needs, and electricity tariff [1]. For essential-load backup, a **10 kWh battery** may cover several hours of critical loads, while whole-home backup often requires **20–30 kWh** [2].

---

## What to look for

Choose a system with:

1. **LFP battery chemistry**
   LFP batteries are now widely preferred for home storage because they are safer, long-lasting, and tolerate deeper discharge better than older chemistries [2].

2. **At least 10-year warranty**
   Most home batteries have warranties around 10 years, and many are expected to last around 10–12 years depending on usage [1].

3. **High round-trip efficiency**
   Look for 90%+ efficiency. Tesla Powerwall 3 and MidNite Powerflo 16 are listed at **97.5%**, while Sigenergy SigenStor is listed at **98%** [4].

4. **Enough power output**
   Capacity, measured in kWh, tells you how long the battery lasts. Power output, measured in kW, tells you what appliances it can run at once.

5. **Backup capability included**
   Not every solar + battery system automatically powers your home during a blackout; you need the right inverter, backup gateway, and wiring [3].

## Estimated cost

Battery costs vary by country and installer. In the U.S., a single **10–13.5 kWh battery system** often costs roughly **$10,000–$15,000 installed before incentives** [2]. In the UK, battery storage can range from about **£1,500 to £10,000**, with a 5 kWh system around **£4,600** [1]. In Australia, after rebates, a **10 kWh battery** may cost around **$7,000–$11,000 installed** [3].

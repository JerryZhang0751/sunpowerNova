Home solar batteries typically last **5–15 years**, with most modern lithium batteries lasting around **10–15 years** [1][2]. That means you’ll likely replace the battery **at least once** during a solar panel system’s 25–30 year lifespan [1][2].

Typical lifespans by battery type:

- **Lithium iron phosphate (LFP/LiFePO₄):** about **10–15+ years** [1]
- **Lithium-ion/NMC:** about **8–12 years** [1]
- **Lead-acid:** about **3–7 years**, often shorter with frequent use [1][4]

How long yours lasts depends mainly on:

- **How often it cycles** — daily use wears it faster than backup-only use [2]
- **Depth of discharge** — regularly draining it deeply can shorten its life [2][4]
- **Temperature** — extreme heat or cold accelerates degradation [2][4]
- **Battery chemistry and quality** — lithium batteries generally last longer than lead-acid [2][4]

As a rule of thumb: a battery used mostly for **backup** may last **15+ years**, daily solar self-consumption is often **12–15 years**, and heavy off-grid use may be closer to **8–12 years** [1].

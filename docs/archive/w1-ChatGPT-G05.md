For a **detached house in Spain**, the usual best-value setup is:

> **4–6 kWp solar PV + 5–10 kWh battery + hybrid inverter**, sized around your daytime use, evening use, roof space, and whether you have an EV, pool pump, electric heating/AC or heat pump.

## 1. Typical system sizes

| Home type | Suggested PV | Suggested battery | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Small/efficient home, 2–3 people | 3–4 kWp | 5 kWh | Good if no EV/heat pump |
| Average detached house | 5–6 kWp | 5–10 kWh | Most common sweet spot |
| Large home, pool, AC, heat pump | 7–10 kWp | 10–15 kWh | Needs careful sizing |
| EV charging regularly | 7–12 kWp | 10 kWh+ optional | Better if EV charges in daytime |

In Spain, oversizing purely to export is usually not ideal, because exported surplus is credited at a lower value than electricity you use directly.

## 2. Why a battery makes sense in Spain

Spain’s self-consumption rules reward using your own solar power more than exporting it. Under **compensación simplificada**, surplus solar is credited on your bill, but it is **not a full 1:1 net-metering system**, there is **no cash payout**, and credits reset monthly [1]. Exported energy is typically valued much lower than retail electricity: roughly **€0.04–€0.12/kWh** depending on hour/season, while household electricity may cost around **€0.18–€0.28/kWh** [1].

That means a battery can improve economics by storing midday solar for evening use. A cited Madrid example showed a 5 kWp system increasing self-consumption from about **40% without battery** to around **65% with a 5 kWh battery** [1].

## 3. Indicative installed cost

Very rough market ranges for a detached house:

| System | Approx. installed cost |
|---|---:|
| 5 kWp solar only | €5,500–€8,000 |
| 5 kWp solar + 5 kWh battery | €8,500–€12,500 |
| 6–8 kWp solar + 10 kWh battery | €12,000–€18,000 |
| Backup/emergency power option | +€500–€2,000 typically |

Actual cost depends heavily on roof type, scaffolding, inverter brand, battery brand, electrical panel work, and whether backup circuits are included.

## 4. Payback expectation

For a well-sized detached-house system in Spain:

- **Solar only:** often around **5–8 years**
- **Solar + battery:** often around **7–10 years**
- Better payback if you have high evening consumption, high electricity prices, a pool pump, air conditioning, heat pump, or EV charging.
- Grants, tax deductions and local incentives can shorten payback substantially.

One Madrid model for a **5 kWp system with 5 kWh battery** estimated about **€1,074/year annual benefit** and around **7-year simple payback** after a 30% grant assumption [1]. Treat that as an example, not a quote.

## 5. Subsidies and tax deductions

Spain’s incentives vary by **autonomous community** and municipality. Some support may include:

- Regional grants for solar and storage
- Municipal **IBI** or **ICIO** tax reductions
- Possible **IRPF** deductions for energy-efficiency improvements
- Local programmes depending on region and budget availability

Holaluz notes that grants depend on the autonomous community and that **IRPF deductions** may still help even if direct subsidies are unavailable [2]. Recent market commentary also suggests tax incentives can be more meaningful than surplus compensation for many households [3]. Always check your region before signing, because funds open, close and deplete quickly.

## 6. Important legal/technical points in Spain

For grid-connected residential solar, you normally want:

- **Autoconsumo con excedentes acogido a compensación simplificada**
- Certified installer
- **CIE / Certificado de Instalación Eléctrica**
- Registration with your autonomous community
- DSO notification and bidirectional smart meter activation

Spain’s system is based on **Real Decreto 244/2019**, which created the current self-consumption framework and simplified surplus compensation [1]. Registration and DSO activation can take several weeks; one source gives typical registration/activation times of roughly **2–8 weeks**, depending on region and distributor [1].

## 7. Battery backup: check this carefully

A normal solar + battery system **does not always power your house during a blackout**. If you want backup, ask specifically for:

- Hybrid inverter with backup/EPS function
- Backup box or automatic transfer switch
- Dedicated critical-load circuit: fridge, lights, router, garage door, etc.
- Whether it supports three-phase backup if your home is three-phase
- Whether solar can recharge the battery during an outage

Without this, the system may shut down during grid failure for safety.

## 8. What I would ask installers for

Ask for at least **3 quotes** with the same assumptions:

1. Annual household consumption in kWh
2. Hourly or monthly consumption profile
3. Roof orientation, tilt, shading study
4. PV size in kWp
5. Battery usable capacity in kWh
6. Estimated self-consumption percentage
7. Estimated export compensation
8. Whether backup is included
9. Warranty: panels, inverter, battery, workmanship
10. Subsidy/tax support and who handles paperwork

## My practical recommendation

For an average detached house in Spain, I would start by comparing:

- **Option A:** 5–6 kWp solar only
- **Option B:** 5–6 kWp solar + 5 kWh battery
- **Option C:** 6–8 kWp solar + 10 kWh battery, especially if you have high evening use, EV, pool, or heat pump

If you want resilience as well as savings, make sure the quote includes **true backup capability**, not just a battery.

To run your home on solar **at night**, you need a way to store or draw electricity when panels aren’t producing. Solar panels only generate power in daylight.

## Your options

### 1. Solar + battery storage
This is the main way to use solar at night.

You need:

- Solar panels
- Hybrid or battery inverter
- Home battery system
- Backup load panel or whole-home backup setup
- Transfer switch / proper safety equipment

During the day, your panels power the house and charge the battery. At night, your home runs from the battery.

### 2. Grid-tied solar without batteries
If you have net metering, your solar exports extra power during the day, and you use grid power at night. Your bill is offset, but your house is not literally running on solar at night.

This is usually cheaper than batteries.

### 3. Solar + battery + grid backup
This is often the best setup. The battery handles nighttime and outages, while the grid remains available if the battery runs low.

### 4. Off-grid solar system
This requires much larger batteries and solar capacity. It’s more expensive because the system must handle cloudy days, winter, heavy loads, and backup needs.

---

## How to size the battery

Look at your electric bill and find your average daily usage in kWh.

Example:

- Home uses 30 kWh/day
- Nighttime usage is maybe 40–60% of that
- You may need roughly 12–20 kWh of usable battery capacity for one night

Common home batteries are around 10–15 kWh each, so many homes need 1–2 batteries for nighttime use, and more for whole-home backup.

---

## Important: big appliances matter

Running lights, fridge, Wi-Fi, TVs, and outlets is easy.

Running these at night requires a much larger battery/inverter:

- Central AC
- Electric heat
- Electric water heater
- EV charger
- Electric stove/oven
- Well pump
- Dryer

You may choose either:

- **Essential-load backup**: fridge, lights, internet, some outlets
- **Whole-home backup**: everything, including large appliances

Whole-home backup costs much more.

---

## Basic setup recommendation

For most homes, I’d recommend:

1. Grid-tied solar system
2. Hybrid inverter
3. 10–30 kWh battery storage
4. Essential-load backup panel
5. Optional generator backup if you need long outage protection

---

## Quick rule of thumb

If you just want nighttime solar use:

- **Small home / essential loads:** 10–15 kWh battery
- **Average home:** 15–30 kWh battery
- **Large home or AC at night:** 30+ kWh battery
- **Off-grid:** often 40–100+ kWh plus oversized solar

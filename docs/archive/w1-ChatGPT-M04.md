### What to look for in reviews

When comparing home solar and storage companies, prioritize reviews that mention:

- **Installation quality** — clean wiring, roof work, permitting, and inspection results
- **Post-install support** — how quickly they respond to monitoring, inverter, or battery issues
- **Warranty handling** — whether the installer actually helps with claims
- **Battery expertise** — not all solar installers are equally good at storage design
- **Transparent pricing** — avoid companies that rely on high-pressure sales or unclear financing
- **Local reputation** — solar is highly local because permitting, utility rules, and incentive knowledge matter

EnergySage says battery selection should be based on performance, warranty, system intelligence, value, and popularity among installers [4]. For home batteries, storage is especially useful if your area has weak net metering, frequent outages, or you want more independence from the utility [4].

### Battery sizing matters

Most homes do **not** need the largest battery available. A European sizing guide suggests many households with rooftop solar need about **5–15 kWh** of usable battery capacity, depending on usage, solar size, and electricity tariff [3]. A typical four-person household may need around **8–10 kWh** for solar self-consumption, or **10–13 kWh** if using dynamic/time-of-use rates [3].

For most homeowners, I’d shortlist:

- **Tesla Powerwall 3** if you want the most mainstream, highly rated all-around battery.

Typically:

- **Solar panels only:** about **5–10 years** to pay for themselves, often faster where electricity rates are high or incentives are strong. One example calculation for a 10 kW system showed about **~5 years** payback [1].
- **Solar + home battery:** often about **7–12+ years**, because the battery adds a lot of upfront cost. In the same example, adding a 10 kWh battery increased payback to about **~7 years** [1].
- **Battery added mainly for backup:** it may **not “pay for itself” quickly** financially; its value is often resilience, blackout protection, and using more of your own solar at night.

The simple formula is:

**Payback period = upfront cost after incentives ÷ annual bill savings**

Your annual savings depend mainly on your electricity price, how much solar you use at home, export/net-metering rates, system size, installation cost, and incentives [1][2]. Batteries improve “self-consumption” — often around **60–80% with a battery** — but if the battery is oversized, it can actually lengthen the payback period [2].

As a rough example: if solar + battery costs **$25,000 after incentives** and saves **$3,000/year**, payback is:

**$25,000 ÷ $3,000 = about 8.3 years**
